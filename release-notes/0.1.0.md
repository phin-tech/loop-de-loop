## LoopDeLoop

A menu-bar app for the Loupedeck Live, for when the official software won't update your device and other tools won't
support its firmware. This is the first release. It talks to the device directly over USB, with no Loupedeck software
needed, and is tested on firmware 0.2.5.

- **Design your keys.** Give each of the 12 touch keys a title, colours and a picture: a symbol from a searchable
  picker of thousands of SF Symbols, or your own image.
- **Actions on everything.** Every key, knob and round button can do something when you press it, when you hold it
  and when you let go. Knobs also act when you turn them left or right, and their labels show on the side strips.
  Actions include keyboard shortcuts, typed text, media keys, opening apps and URLs, shell commands, AppleScript,
  Shortcuts, and switching page or profile.
- **Keyboard Maestro, BetterTouchTool and Alfred.** Run a Keyboard Maestro macro (picked from a list, and passed a
  value if you like), fire a BetterTouchTool named trigger, or call an Alfred workflow's external trigger.
- **Pages and profiles.** As many pages as you like in each profile, and profiles that switch with the frontmost app.
- **Round buttons.** Set the colour of each button's light.
- **A `loopctl` command and a URL scheme.** Scripts and macros can change keys, labels, button colours, pages and
  brightness while LoopDeLoop runs: `loopctl set-key 3 --title "On air" --bg ff0000`, or
  `open -g 'loopdeloop://page/next'`. Settings has a button to install it, and `brew install --cask loopdeloop` does
  it too.
- **Export and import.** Share a profile, images included, as one file. Take full backups and restore them.
- **Sturdy.** Firmware 0.2.5 can lock up on certain images. LoopDeLoop notices and reconnects within milliseconds, so
  you shouldn't see it. It also reconnects after sleep and when you plug the device back in.
- **Updates.** LoopDeLoop keeps itself up to date, and starts at login.

LoopDeLoop needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later. Keyboard shortcuts, media keys and typed text need the Accessibility
permission. The first time an action talks to Keyboard Maestro, BetterTouchTool or Alfred, macOS asks whether
LoopDeLoop may control it.

LoopDeLoop's Loupedeck protocol is ported from foxxyz/loupedeck (MIT licensed). Loupedeck is a trademark of Logitech,
which isn't involved with LoopDeLoop.
